Maynardville Festival Operations Platform — User Guide  
*Your friendly sidekick for complimentary ticketing, approvals, and festival insights.*

---

## 1. Welcome / What this system does

Running a festival is a beautiful whirlwind, and complimentary tickets (we’ll call them **comps** for short) can easily become a tangle of emails, spreadsheets and sticky notes. The Maynardville Festival Operations Platform gives everyone a single, calm place to request, approve, issue and report on comps. It connects your team — Leadership, Box Office, PR, Sponsorship and Operations — and shows each department exactly what they need, without noise. You can use it from any device (laptop, tablet or phone) with a modern browser — no apps to install, just sign in with your work email and you’re ready.

## 3. Signing in

**Staff members**  
Go to the platform’s sign-in page and enter your Maynardville work email address. You’ll receive an email with a secure, one-time link — click it and you’re in. There’s no password to remember or share. Your session stays active for about 12 hours, so you can pick up where you left off through the day.

**Requesters**  
You don’t sign in at all. You are given a private link (unique to you) by the festival team. Simply open that link whenever you need to submit a comp request. The link is yours — please don’t share it, because it identifies you and controls which categories appear on your form.

## 4. The complimentary-ticket journey (end to end)

Every comp request moves through a clear life cycle. The **status** of a request tells everyone exactly where it stands. Here are the statuses you’ll see:

| Status | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **REQUEST** | The requester has submitted their form. It’s waiting for Leadership to review. |
| **TO ISSUE** | Leadership has approved the request. The Box Office now needs to draw and record the tickets. |
| **ISSUED** | The tickets have been issued — seat numbers and a Quicket reference are recorded. They now form part of the official comp list. |
| **DECLINED** | The request was not approved. A clear reason is shown to the requester. |
| **CANCELLED** | The request is no longer active (cancelled by Leadership or withdrawn). Typically used if something changes before tickets are issued. |

Now let’s walk through the journey step by step:

1. **A requester submits their request.**  
   They open their private link, choose a performance and the comp category they’re allowed, add guest names and details, and submit. The system immediately creates the request with the status **REQUEST**. An email notification goes to Leadership.

2. **Leadership reviews the request.**  
   Jaco or Wessel opens the Approvals screen and sees the request in a queue. They can read the details and decide to:  
   - **Approve** — the status changes to **TO ISSUE**. The Box Office and the requester are both notified.  
   - **Decline** — they choose a reason and the status becomes **DECLINED**. Only the requester receives the outcome.

3. **The Box Office prepares the tickets.**  
   Jeff sees all approved requests on the “To Issue” tab. He draws the actual tickets in Quicket (the festival’s ticketing platform), then fills in:  
   - **Seat Numbers** (e.g. A12, A13)  
   - **Ticket Reference** (the Quicket order or ticket number)  
   Once both fields are completed, he clicks **Issue**. The system won’t allow the ticket to be marked ISSUED without both; it will gently prompt if something is missing.

4. **Tickets become part of the Full Comps List.**  
   After successful issuing, the request moves to status **ISSUED** and appears on the protected Full Comps List. Only Leadership can edit this list after issue — it’s the festival’s permanent record. The requester receives an email letting them know their tickets are ready.

If a request is no longer needed before step 3, Leadership can cancel it and the status moves to CANCELLED, removing it from the active pipeline.

## 5. Getting around (navigation)

When you sign in, you’ll always see a clean top bar:

- **Maynardville logo (top-left)** — clicking it returns you to your Dashboard hub from anywhere in the system.
- **Navigation menu** — shows a list of the dashboards and tools you have access to. For example, Jeff might see “Box Office” and “Sales vs Comp Report”, while Sascha sees “PR & Media Dashboard”.
- **Sign out (top-right)** — ends your session. We recommend signing out if you’re on a shared computer.

Your **Dashboard hub** is your launchpad. It displays a set of cards, each one representing an area you can open — like “Approvals”, “Box Office”, “Festival Leadership Dashboard”, or your departmental dashboard. Click any card to dive in.

## 6. Screen-by-screen guide

### Requester form (personal link)
When a requester opens their private link, they see a simple form that belongs only to them. They fill in:

- Guest first name and surname
- Performance (a dropdown of current festival dates)
- Comp category (only the categories they are allowed — for example, a Sponsorship colleague might see “Partner” and “Competition”)
- Guest email address (optional, but helps Leadership)
- Number of seats
- House-seats toggle (a checkbox marked “House seats”) — this simply flags the comp for internal house-use tracking
- Notes (anything the requester wants to add)

They submit the form and immediately see a confirmation message on screen. The request appears in the pipeline and an email is sent. The requester cannot see anyone else’s requests.

### Dashboard hub
After signing in, staff land here. It’s a collection of cards representing the tools available to your role. For example, a Leadership member might see “Approvals”, “Festival Leadership Dashboard”, “Sales vs Comp Report”, etc. Click a card to open that screen.

### Approvals (Leadershipp)
This screen shows a queue of all requests with the status **REQUEST**. Each line lists the requester’s name, guest name, performance, category, number of seats, and any notes. For each request you can:

- Click **Approve** → the status changes to TO ISSUE and notifications are sent.
- Click **Decline** → you’re prompted to provide a short reason (which is shared with the requester), and the request becomes DECLINED.

There is no bulk action — you review and respond one request at a time, which keeps the process thoughtful and personal.

### Box Office
Jeff sees two clear tabs:

**To Issue**  
This tab lists every request in **TO ISSUE** status, showing who the tickets are for, which performance, how many seats, and two empty fields per row: **Seat Numbers** and **Ticket Reference**. He fills in both, then clicks **Issue**. The system checks that both fields are complete, updates the status to ISSUED, and moves the record over to the Full Comps List. If he tries to issue without both fields, the screen will ask him to complete them — nothing slips through.

**Full Comps List**  
A read-only record of every comp ticket that has been issued. It includes all the details: requester, guest, performance, seat numbers, and the Quicket reference. This list is a trusted source of truth for the festival, and only Leadership can make corrections if something needs to change.

### Festival Leadership dashboard
This is the command centre for Jaco and Wessel. It packs a lot into one screen, designed for at-a-glance oversight:

- **Headline numbers** — tallies of total requests made, plus counts for each status: REQUEST, TO ISSUE, ISSUED, and DECLINED.
- **House-seat usage** — a summary of how many tickets have been marked as house seats.
- **Breakdowns** — charts or tables that show comps grouped by performance and by requester, so you can spot patterns quickly.
- **Action needed panel** — highlights any issued tickets that may have incomplete seat details (for example, if a reference was recorded but seat numbers were accidentally blanked out). This acts as a safety net so nothing is overlooked.
- **Quicket sales summary** — a small card showing total tickets sold across the festival and gross revenue, pulled automatically from Quicket. It gives you a live sense of how the festival is performing, right next to your comp figures.

From here you can click through to the full Sales vs Comp Report for a deeper dive.

### PR & Media / Sponsorship / Operations dashboards
Each departmental dashboard follows the same friendly layout as the Leadership dashboard, but the information is filtered to the comp categories that matter to that department. For instance, Sascha’s PR & Media view only reflects Media and VIP comps. The dashboards show request summaries, issued ticket counts, and performance breakdowns — so you can monitor your world without scrolling through everything else. No approval actions here; these are visibility tools.

### Sales vs Comp Report
This separate report (accessible from the menu or dashboard cards) gives you a performance-by-performance breakdown. The table shows:

| Performance | Capacity | Tickets Sold | Gross (ZAR) | Comps Issued | Total Allocated | % of Capacity | Remaining |
|-------------|----------|--------------|-------------|--------------|-----------------|---------------|-----------|

The **Total Allocated** column adds sold tickets and issued comps, and the **% of Capacity** and **Remaining** numbers help you instantly see how full each performance is. A **CSV export** button lets you download the table for offline analysis or sharing.

## 7. Email notifications

The system keeps the right people in the loop automatically. Here’s who gets what:

| Event | Who receives an email |
|-------|-----------------------|
| A new comp request is submitted | Leadership (Jaco, Wessel) |
| Request approved (status TO ISSUE) | Box Office (Jeff) + the requester |
| Request declined (with reason) | The requester |
| Tickets issued (status ISSUED) | The requester |
| Daily reminder: any ISSUED ticket missing seat numbers or ticket reference | Leadership + Box Office |

The daily reminder acts as a gentle nudge — if something was left incomplete, it surfaces quickly.

## 8. Ticket sales from Quicket

The platform connects directly to Quicket so that ticket-sales data arrives on its own. That means you don’t need to copy and paste any sales figures. The moment a member of the public buys a ticket, the sales tally grows. Leadership sees this in the dashboard summary and in the Sales vs Comp Report.

Remember: comp tickets that are drawn directly in Quicket are **recorded by the Box Office in this platform** — the platform is where you assign seat numbers and track the comp officially. The sales and comp information live alongside each other so you can always compare how many seats have been sold against how many have been given as comps, all in one place.

## 9. Everyday admin tasks (brief)

These tasks are managed by Leadership or by a designated admin user. They’re simple in concept, and you’ll receive a separate detailed runbook during handover for the step-by-step instructions.

- **Adding a new performance** — when a new show date is added to the festival programme, Leadership can enter its name, date, time and capacity through the admin panel. The performance then shows up in requesters’ dropdowns and in all reports.
- **Adding or removing a requester** — Leadership can create a new requester record, decide which comp categories that person may request, and generate their unique personal link. Removing or suspending a requester is equally straightforward.
- **Rolling the system over to a new festival season** — the platform can be prepared for next year: archive the previous season’s data, set up the new programme and requester list, and start fresh. The handover runbook walks you through it all.

If you ever need to do something that isn’t obvious, the runbook is your reference.

## 10. Your data & privacy

All the information in the platform belongs to the Maynardville Open-Air Festival. No third party has access unless you explicitly authorise it. Access is tightly controlled by role — nobody sees personal guest details unless their job genuinely requires it. Guest names and email addresses are visible to Leadership, the Box Office and, in anonymised summaries, to departmental heads — never to other requesters or the public.

The system was designed with South Africa’s Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) in mind. We collect only what’s needed, keep it safe, and you can export or delete data whenever you wish. If you have any privacy questions, talk to Leadership — the data is yours, always.
